* Maybe have both the time and the time offset ("22:30:30" and
  "-1m30s"?)
* Have a mode where you can simply state a final endpoint and pingem will
  choose the midpoints to metric. Anyone (particularly gamers) who wanted to
  see how their network connections were behaving (maybe they feel they need to
  change providers) would love to know where the blame for their latency and
  loss issues goes.
* Mousing over a loss event (a run of lost packets) should give per-event
  info: start time, duration, loss percentage over that span, and anything
  else interesting about the event.
* In the "by flow" view, merge the histograms of flows whose routes are
  identical, since they're really one path.
* A map view of the routers' locations (geolocation), alongside the route
  graph.
* When the route graph's layout is redone (new routes, or hosts added or
  removed), morph the nodes to their new places over a second or two instead
  of jumping, so the eye can follow.
* Add an estimated per-hop time to each line on the map?
* All-routes map: fit the whole map on screen in a tiny layout, with the
  area around the mouse magnified (a fisheye), instead of dragging.
* To discuss: compare hosts against each other. When several hosts spike or
  lose packets at the same moment, highlight the furthest-out link they all
  share (the problem is in common: Wi-Fi, router, ISP head end); when only
  one does, highlight the part of its path that's its own. Works for short
  events, since it uses the pings. Needs a definition of "an event".
* To discuss (after per-link time and loss): label who owns each stretch of
  the map (comcast.net, ntt.net, level3.net... from reverse DNS), so "+90 ms,
  3% loss here" becomes "it's NTT's Pacific link".
